# Validation of the WP M equations against the NASA Lift+Cruise reference vehicle

**Purpose.** Before the independent checker (`methods/check_design.py`) is used on E8-200, its equations are run on the
one published, fully documented lift + cruise eVTOL design study: the NASA RVLT battery-electric Lift+Cruise concept
vehicle (eight 10 ft lift rotors, one pusher, six occupants / 1,200 lb payload). Results table generated by
`methods/nasa_lpc_reproduction.py`: [nasa_lpc_reproduction_table.md](nasa_lpc_reproduction_table.md) (data in
`nasa_lpc_reproduction.json`).

**Published data used.**
- [S18] C. Silva, W. Johnson, K. Antcliff, M. Patterson, *VTOL Urban Air Mobility Concept Vehicles for Technology
  Development*, AIAA 2018-3847 (doi 10.2514/6.2018-3847): Table 1 (mission), Table 3 column "L+C E" (all vehicle data),
  sec. VI (battery 650 -> 530 -> 400 Wh/kg, motor eq. 1 and 1.63 accessory factor).
  https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/silva2018.pdf
- [JS22] W. Johnson, C. Silva, *NASA concept vehicles and the engineering of advanced air mobility aircraft*,
  The Aeronautical Journal 126 (2022), Table 3 column "Lift+Cruise Electric" (re-sized vehicle).
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210026170/downloads/1521_Johnson%20%26%20Silva_122721%20.pdf

Not published (so not reproducible): wing span/area, pusher diameter/rpm, rotor-support and wing weights, the
segment-by-segment energy. These are noted where they limit the comparison.

## Conclusions

1. **Hover physics and battery bookkeeping reproduce NASA to within ±5 %** (disk loading, hover power per lifter with
   NASA's FM, hover and cruise C-rates, usable battery energy). The checker's blade-element FM (0.68 vs 0.74) is the
   main source of difference and errs on the conservative side; when D supplies a measured or CFD-based FM, the checker
   reports both.
2. **Motor mass**: NDARC eq. (1) with the 1.63 accessory factor reproduces NASA's propulsion group to +12 % (2018);
   the +21 % on the 2022 vehicle comes from not knowing NASA's torque sizing condition. Against 7 COTS motors in
   `bom/catalog.json` the same equation is within ±8 % for 5 of 7 (REB 30 +32 %, REB 90 -23 %).
3. **Mission energy** matches (-3 %) only if NASA's "energy burn" is the two-flight mission without the 20-minute reserve
   (the paper also states that usable energy at 400 Wh/kg equals the energy burn, which is consistent with the battery
   being sized to it). The reserve accounting is therefore the main ambiguity of the reproduction, not the cruise or
   hover physics.
4. **Rotor-group and structure weights cannot be validated** from the published data (no breakdown, no wing geometry);
   S18 itself states that its boom/rotor-support weights are "likely quite optimistic" and JS22 reports that higher-
   fidelity structural analysis found the lift+cruise structure optimistically light. The checker's boom model [M10]
   is therefore physics-based and must be confirmed by WP F.

Overall accuracy: **hover power, C-rates and battery energy within ±5 %; motor/propulsion mass +12 to +22 %; mission
energy -3 % (with the stated reserve reading)**. The checker tolerances in `methods/EQUATIONS.md` sec. 11 (±10 % warn /
±20 % fail on power and energy, ±25 % / ±50 % on components) are consistent with this accuracy.
